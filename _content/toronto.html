Toronto is a load-in problem, not a distance problem

We are twenty-five minutes up the 400 from downtown, so getting to a Toronto event has never been the hard part. Getting a booth, a backdrop and a prop case into the actual room is. Suburban banquet halls are designed for vendors; downtown buildings are designed for tenants, and the difference costs an unprepared vendor an hour they do not have.

Below is every constraint we actually hit in this city, and what we do about each one. If you send us the building when you enquire, we handle all of it before the day.

The loading dock has a booking window

Most downtown venues and office towers require a dock slot reserved in advance, often in one-hour blocks, and often not on the day you want. We ask for the venue address at enquiry rather than at confirmation so there is time to get a slot that actually lines up with your start.

The freight elevator is not the passenger elevator

Booth cases do not go up in a passenger lift during a live event, and most buildings will not let you try. Freight is usually bookable alongside the dock, sometimes needs an operator, and sometimes stops running at a fixed hour in the evening. That last one matters for teardown as much as setup: we need to know we can get out.

Security wants names in advance

A desk that has no record of us is the single most common way a load-in stalls. We send our attendant's name ahead when the building asks for it.

Union houses have their own rules

Some Toronto venues require in-house labour to move equipment, or restrict what an outside vendor may touch. It is entirely workable and it is normal, but it needs to be known beforehand rather than discovered at the dock. Tell us the venue and we will ask the question for you.

There is nowhere to park

We plan for this. It is our problem, not yours, and we do not bill parking back to you.

Some venues need a certificate of insurance

We carry commercial liability insurance and can issue a certificate naming your venue as additional insured. Ask early, because some buildings will not release a dock booking without one on file.

The three kinds of Toronto booking

Corporate, downtown. Office parties, conferences, brand activations and university events. These are our most common Toronto job and the reason the logistics above are written down. Branding, green screen and template detail sit on the corporate page. Guests arrive across a wide window rather than all at once, so three or four hours usually fits better than two.

Weddings, across the city. Toronto weddings vary far more than Vaughan's do, from a small east-end restaurant buyout to a full hotel ballroom. The open-air booth suits nearly all of them because it scales down without looking sparse. Where the room is heavily designed, the glam booth avoids clashing with it, since black and white does not fight anyone's palette.

Condo party rooms and private celebrations. Birthdays, anniversaries, engagement parties. The service elevator booking is the whole game here; the rooms themselves are almost always fine for a standard booth.

Etobicoke, North York, Scarborough and East York

All four are part of the City of Toronto and all four are covered by this page at the same rates. We have deliberately not built separate pages for them: they share these venues, these buildings and these constraints, and a page that could be produced by swapping the neighbourhood name is not worth your time or Google's.

What does change across the city is the building type. The old boroughs skew toward ground-level banquet halls, community centres and golf clubs, which behave much more like our Vaughan venues: easy access, a service door, parking you can back into. The closer you get to the core, the more the dock-and-freight routine above applies.
