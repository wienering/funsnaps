A Markham night, hour by hour

Markham runs two very different calendars. Large multicultural weddings fill the banquet rooms at weekends, and the tech and business corridor along Highway 404 fills weekday evenings with corporate events. What the two have in common is a tight programme, and a booth that ignores the programme wastes your money.

So instead of a feature list, here is what actually happens on the night. Times assume a 6pm start for a wedding reception; shift everything for a corporate night.

4:00pm — the van arrives

About two hours before doors for a large room, an hour for a smaller one. We find the service entrance, check in with the venue coordinator and work out where the booth will not be in the way of catering. This is unpaid time on your invoice: your booked hours have not started.

4:30pm — backdrop and booth up

Backdrop stand, booth, prop table, lighting. Then the part most people never think about: every cable gets taped flat. A trailing cable across a banquet-hall walkway is how a guest goes down, and no photo is worth that.

5:15pm — test strips

We run real prints through the dye-sub printer and check the template, the crop and the colour under the actual room lighting. If your template has custom art on it, this is where a stretched logo gets caught rather than at 8pm in front of a queue.

6:00pm — doors, and the booth opens

Cocktail hour is the booth's strongest window at a Markham wedding. Guests are standing, holding a drink, and have nothing scheduled. Expect the heaviest print volume of the night here.

7:00pm — dinner, and the booth goes quiet

Once people are seated for a plated dinner, use drops to almost nothing. This is the single most common way couples waste booked hours. If your package is three hours, spending one of them on dinner service is throwing a third of it away. We would rather close the booth here and reopen later.

8:00pm — speeches and traditions

Still quiet, and at a large Markham reception the programme can run considerably longer than planned. Tea ceremonies, multiple speeches and family traditions all take the room's attention, which is exactly where it should be.

9:00pm — dance floor, and the second rush

The booth's other peak. Groups get bigger and louder, props get used properly, and the queue becomes self-sustaining because people can see it. The open-air setup matters here: about a dozen guests fit in one frame and whole tables come up together.

11:00pm — last prints

The attendant gives a visible warning before the booked hours end so nobody arrives to a closed booth. Every strip that has been printed is in guests' hands.

11:15pm — teardown

Also not on your invoice. Cables up, cases packed, floor left as we found it. Your full gallery lands within about a day.

What this means for your hours

The lesson from the timeline is that a booth has two peaks and a long trough in the middle. Booking a continuous block straight through dinner and speeches buys you an idle booth for a third of the time.
